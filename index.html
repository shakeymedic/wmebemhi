The Initial Assessment Pathway

Effective management starts with a rapid, systematic primary survey to identify and address life-threatening conditions, preventing secondary brain injury. This adheres to ATLS principles.

Interactive CT Scan Decision Hub

Judicious use of CT imaging is critical. Use this tool to explore the NICE guidelines for determining which patients require a scan and within what timeframe. Select a population to view the specific criteria.

Scan within 1 hour if ANY factor present:

  • GCS < 13 on initial assessment
  • GCS < 15 at 2 hours post-injury
  • Suspected open or depressed skull fracture
  • Any sign of basal skull fracture (e.g., panda eyes)
  • Post-traumatic seizure
  • Focal neurological deficit
  • More than one episode of vomiting

Scan within 8 hours if LOC or amnesia AND:

  • Age 65 years or over
  • History of bleeding or clotting disorders
  • Dangerous mechanism of injury (e.g., fall >1m)
  • >30 mins retrograde amnesia before injury

Note: Also consider a scan within 8 hours for patients on anticoagulants/antiplatelets without LOC or amnesia.

Scan within 1 hour if ANY factor present:

  • Suspicion of non-accidental injury
  • Post-traumatic seizure (no epilepsy hx)
  • GCS < 14 (or < 15 if < 1yr)
  • GCS < 15 at 2 hours post-injury
  • Suspected open/depressed skull fracture or tense fontanelle
  • Any sign of basal skull fracture
  • Focal neurological deficit
  • Bruise/swelling/laceration >5cm (if < 1yr)

Observe for 4 hours if ONLY ONE of these:

  • Witnessed LOC > 5 minutes
  • Abnormal drowsiness
  • ≥3 discrete episodes of vomiting
  • Dangerous mechanism of injury
  • Amnesia > 5 minutes
  • Any bleeding or clotting disorder

If deterioration occurs during observation (e.g., further vomiting, GCS drops), scan within 1 hour.

Spotlight on Vulnerable Populations

Specific patient groups require tailored assessment and management strategies due to their unique physiological characteristics and risk profiles.

Paediatric Care & Clinical Decision Rules

To minimise radiation in children, clinical decision rules (CDRs) help identify those at very low risk of significant injury. Studies show high sensitivity for rules like CHALICE and PECARN in detecting clinically important traumatic brain injuries (ciTBI).

Anticoagulated Patients: High Risk

Patients on anticoagulants or antiplatelets have a much higher risk of intracranial haemorrhage, even from minor trauma, necessitating a low threshold for CT scanning and a clear plan for rapid reversal if bleeding is confirmed.

Warfarin Reversal:

IV Vitamin K + Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (PCC).

DOAC Reversal:

Specific agents (Andexanet Alfa, Idarucizumab) or PCC.

Antiplatelet Management:

No specific reversal. Consider platelet transfusion for critical bleeding.

Falls Decision Rule: Older Adults

This rule aims to safely reduce unnecessary head CT scans in older adults (≥65) after ground-level falls. A recent validation study in the Canadian Emergency Medicine Journal (May 2025) confirmed its high sensitivity (98.0%) and negative predictive value (99.6%), potentially reducing CTs by ~32%.

Consider NO CT if ALL criteria met:

  • No history of head injury on falling (patient/witness).
  • No new neurological abnormalities.
  • Patient recalls events surrounding the fall (no amnesia).
  • Clinical Frailty Scale (CFS) score less than 5.

The Future: Blood Biomarkers

The emergence of blood tests to detect brain-specific proteins (GFAP & UCH-L1) has the potential to revolutionise mild head injury assessment, helping to rule out significant injury without imaging.
